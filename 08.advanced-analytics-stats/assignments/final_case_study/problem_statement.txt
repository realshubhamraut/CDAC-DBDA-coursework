Consumer Lending Risk Insights Through Data-Driven Analytics

1. Background & Business Context
Consumer finance companies face significant challenges when lending money to individuals with limited, insufficient, or non-existent credit history. A lack of credit information increases the uncertainty involved in predicting whether a customer will repay the loan on time.
Loan defaults lead to direct financial losses and increase the company’s risk exposure. Conversely, incorrectly rejecting a potentially good customer results in lost business opportunities. Therefore, data-driven decision-making is essential for:
Reducing credit risk


Improving loan approval strategies


Identifying high-risk applicants early


Setting appropriate credit limits and interest rates


Your role is to perform Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) and Statistical Hypothesis Testing to uncover the drivers of loan default using real-world customer data.
The assignment simulates work typically done by analysts in risk management teams at banks, NBFCs, and fintech lenders.

2. Objective of the Case Study
The main aim is to:
Understand the customer attributes and loan characteristics
Identify behavioral and demographic variables that drive default
Discover patterns that differentiate “defaulters” from “non-defaulters”
Use hypothesis testing to validate whether observed patterns are statistically significant
Provide actionable recommendations that reduce credit risk
You must work with two provided datasets that capture customer information at the time of applying for the loan and their previous loan histories.

3. Dataset Description
You are provided with three files:

3.1 Dataset 1 — credit_risk_applicants.csv
This dataset includes information about current loan applicants and contains:
a. Target variable
TARGET = 1 → The client had payment difficulties (defaulted or significantly delayed installment payments)


TARGET = 0 → The client repaid on time


b. Demographics & Personal Information
CODE_GENDER (Gender)


DAYS_BIRTH (Age in days; negative values)


NAME_EDUCATION_TYPE


NAME_FAMILY_STATUS


CNT_CHILDREN


CNT_FAM_MEMBERS


c. Financial Variables
AMT_INCOME_TOTAL (Annual Income)


AMT_CREDIT (Loan amount requested)


AMT_ANNUITY


AMT_GOODS_PRICE


d. Employment & History
DAYS_EMPLOYED


OCCUPATION_TYPE


FLAG_OWN_CAR, FLAG_OWN_REALTY


e. Risk & External Score Features
EXT_SOURCE_1, EXT_SOURCE_2, EXT_SOURCE_3
 These are external credit bureau or scoring system indicators.


f. Housing & Living Conditions
Variables such as:
APARTMENTS_AVG, YEARS_BUILD_AVG, LIVINGAREA_AVG, etc.
 provide information on the living conditions and real estate characteristics.


These variables may indicate stability or affordability.

3.2 Dataset 2 — credit_risk_previous_loans.csv
This dataset contains details about credit risk and previous loan applications made by the same customers (SK_ID_CURR is the linking key).
Key attributes include:
NAME_CONTRACT_STATUS (Approved, Refused, Cancelled, Unused Offer)


AMT_CREDIT (Credit applied previously)


AMT_APPLICATION


AMT_DOWN_PAYMENT


CNT_PAYMENT (Installment count)


NAME_CASH_LOAN_PURPOSE


RATE_INTEREST_PRIMARY


DAYS_DECISION (How many days before the current application the decision was made)


This dataset helps analyze patterns such as:
Repeated rejections


High-risk behavior


History of applying for multiple loans


Past loan outcomes that may influence future defaults



3.3 Dataset 3 — credit_risk_metadata.csv
This file explains the meaning, data type, and context of every variable.
Students are expected to use it for:
Understanding variable significance


Choosing appropriate plots and transformations


Interpreting results accurately



4. Business Challenge
You are hired as a Data Analyst in the Credit Risk Department of a lending company. Your task is to analyze applicant profiles to assist in the following major business goals:

4.1 Reduce the Risk of Approving High-Risk Customers
Identifying attributes that strongly correlate with defaults helps minimize bad loans.

4.2 Avoid Rejecting Creditworthy Customers
If good customers are mistakenly classified as risky, the business loses revenue.

4.3 Recommend Data-Driven Strategies
Based on your analytical findings, the company expects actionable recommendations on:
Loan approval criteria


Customer profiling


Interest rate adjustments


Required documentation


Employment verification policies



5. Analytical Tasks Expected from Students
You must perform the following structured analysis steps:

5.1 Data Cleaning
Identify missing values


Handle outliers


Detect anomalies in variables (e.g., impossible values in DAYS_EMPLOYED)


Decide which features to retain or discard based on business utility



5.2 Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
Univariate Analysis
Study distribution patterns of:
Income


Credit amount


Age


Annuity


Education


Employment length


Bivariate Analysis
Compare defaulters vs non-defaulters across:
Credit amount


Income groups


Housing type


Occupation type


External scores


Multivariate Analysis
Observe relationships among:
Income × Credit × Default


Age × Occupation × Default


Housing conditions × External scores


Correlation Analysis
Identify the strongest relationships among numeric variables and interpret their significance.

5.3 Hypothesis Testing
Students must formulate and test hypotheses such as:
Do defaulters have significantly lower income than non-defaulters?


Is the default rate different across genders?


Are education level and default correlated?


Do previous loan rejections predict higher current default probability?


Is the company’s default rate higher than the industry benchmark?


These require statistical tests such as:
T-tests


Z-tests


ANOVA


Chi-square tests


F-tests


Proportion tests


Each test must include:
Null and alternate hypotheses


Choice of test and justification


Assumptions validation


Test statistic and p-value


Final conclusion in plain business language
